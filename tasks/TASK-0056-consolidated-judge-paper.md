Replace the several overlapping paper drafts with one canonical paper built

## Goal

Answer: *When independent judges, from small local models to frontier APIs,
receive identical typed decisions with objective gold labels, how do their
accuracy and coverage differ, and what does accuracy-only reporting hide?*
Report coverage and failure states (provider errors, rate limits, parse
failures, HTTP 402, abstentions) as first-class results, and explain the
Qwen3.8 Flash run-to-run variance from artifacts.

## Scientific decisions

- Core evidence is the 760 blind records shared by EXP-013/014/015/016/017/019/
  022/024/025/027; 25 judge arms plus a majority-label reference.
- Coverage = resolved/760; conditional accuracy = correct/resolved;
  all-record accuracy = correct/760 (unresolved earns nothing). 95% Wilson
  intervals for every arm; exact McNemar on shared resolved records and on all
  760, Holm-corrected over 325 pairs.
- EXP-013 is presented as a per-source breakdown of the same pool, not a
  broader collection (its `records.jsonl` is byte-identical to EXP-014/015).
- EXP-027 local arms are the low end on the same records but a different
  harness (native label scoring); stated in the paper.
- EXP-025 Grok ablation is a robustness/failure-analysis section.
- Qwen variance (84.48% in EXP-013/014 vs 97.36–99.21% later): same records,
  model id and typed spec; EXP-013 used `run_qwen_streaming_retry.py` with
  `max_tokens=128` and `enable_thinking: False`, the later runs sent neither
  (EXP-015/016/022 via `run_grok_luna_qwen_bakeoff.py`) or used a different
  route/prompt with `max_tokens=1024` (EXP-024). EXP-014's Qwen predictions
  are the EXP-013 predictions. Hidden reasoning is inferred (latency), not
  observed (no token usage recorded); stated as a limitation.
- Selective escalation, Hearsay, InferHub provider selection,
  GLEIF/EDGAR/CourtListener, hard negatives and TF-IDF moved to future work;
  calibration kept as a short appendix.
